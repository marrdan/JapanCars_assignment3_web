Why Japanese cars feel different

After the 1970s oil crisis, Japanese makers had a clear advantage: small, efficient engines. Then came the 1980s and 1990s, when money and ambition pushed engineers to build surprising cars: mid-engine hybrids, all-wheel-drive rally replicas, rotary coupes.

Many of those cars were never sold outside Japan, which is why the letters JDM (Japanese Domestic Market) became a badge of honour for fans abroad.

1993 – 2002 · Rear-wheel drive

Nissan Skyline GT-R (R34)

2.6 L twin-turbo six (RB26DETT) with all-wheel drive. Nicknamed "Godzilla".

1999 – 2002 · All-wheel drive

Mazda RX-7 (FD)

Twin-turbo rotary engine with almost perfect weight balance. Light, smooth and loud.

1992 – 2002 · Rear-wheel drive

Honda NSX

Mid-engine V6 with an all-aluminium body. Proved a supercar could be easy to drive daily.

1990 – 2005 · Mid-engine

Mitsubishi Lancer Evolution

A family sedan turned rally weapon: turbo 2.0 L engine and all-wheel drive.

1992 – 2016 · All-wheel drive

Toyota Corolla Levin / Trueno

Small, light, 1.6 L engine. Became the drift icon thanks to street racers and anime.

1983 – 1987 · Rear-wheel drive
